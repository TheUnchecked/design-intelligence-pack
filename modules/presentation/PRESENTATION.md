# Design Intelligence Pack — Presentation Module

## Purpose

Define how the Design Intelligence Master is applied to presentations.

This module applies to:

* executive presentations;
* technical presentations;
* management briefings;
* assessment presentations;
* project updates;
* decision presentations;
* stakeholder briefings.

The module does not define a specific subject matter.

---

## 1. Presentation Objective

A presentation must have a clear communication objective.

Determine whether the presentation is primarily intended to:

* inform;
* explain;
* persuade;
* align;
* recommend;
* request a decision;
* communicate progress;
* communicate risk.

Do not combine multiple primary objectives without establishing their hierarchy.

---

## 2. Audience

Identify:

* decision makers;
* technical stakeholders;
* operational stakeholders;
* business stakeholders;
* subject-matter experts;
* external stakeholders.

Adapt:

* terminology;
* depth;
* evidence;
* narrative;
* visual complexity;

to the audience.

---

## 3. Narrative

A presentation should create a logical progression.

Possible narrative models include:

### Context → Problem → Evidence → Implication → Action

Use when explaining an issue.

### Situation → Insight → Recommendation → Decision

Use when supporting a decision.

### Objective → Analysis → Findings → Recommendation → Next Steps

Use for assessments and analytical work.

### Progress → Results → Issues → Actions → Outlook

Use for status and progress reporting.

The AI must select the narrative according to the objective.

Do not force every presentation into the same structure.

---

## 4. Slide Architecture

A slide should normally communicate one primary idea.

Each slide should have:

* one main message;
* supporting evidence;
* appropriate visual hierarchy;
* optional supporting detail.

### Slide title

The title should communicate the slide's message whenever appropriate.

Prefer:

**"Critical vulnerabilities remain concentrated in externally exposed systems"**

over:

**"Vulnerabilities"**

---

## 5. Information Hierarchy

Prioritize:

1. Main message
2. Key evidence
3. Implication
4. Supporting detail
5. Source or methodology

Do not give every element equal visual weight.

---

## 6. Slide Density

Avoid excessive information density.

If a slide requires substantial explanation to understand its main point, consider:

* simplifying;
* restructuring;
* splitting;
* moving detail to an appendix.

Do not split slides merely to increase slide count.

---

## 7. Visual Design

Apply the Core visual principles:

* hierarchy before detail;
* simplicity before complexity;
* consistency before novelty;
* content drives design;
* accessibility is a requirement.

Use visual elements to improve comprehension.

Suitable patterns include:

* charts;
* diagrams;
* timelines;
* matrices;
* process flows;
* comparisons;
* callouts;
* tables when appropriate.

Do not use visuals merely as decoration.

---

## 8. Charts and Data

Choose the visual representation according to the analytical question.

Examples:

### Comparison

Bar chart.

### Trend

Line chart.

### Composition

Stacked bar or appropriate composition chart.

### Relationship

Scatter plot or relationship diagram.

### Process

Flow diagram.

### Priority

Matrix or ranked visualization.

### Distribution

Histogram or appropriate distribution chart.

### Rule

The chart must make the intended insight easier to understand than the raw data.

Never distort scales or imply precision unsupported by the data.

---

## 9. Executive Communication

For executive audiences:

Prioritize:

* impact;
* decisions;
* priorities;
* business implications;
* actions.

Reduce unnecessary technical detail.

Technical evidence may be retained in:

* supporting slides;
* appendix;
* technical documentation.

Do not remove evidence required to support a conclusion.

---

## 10. Technical Communication

For technical audiences:

Include sufficient:

* evidence;
* methodology;
* technical context;
* dependencies;
* assumptions;
* limitations.

Maintain hierarchy so that technical depth does not obscure the main message.

---

## 11. Brand Application

When a Brand Guidelines document is available:

* use the defined typography;
* use approved colors;
* respect approved gradients;
* follow visual identity rules;
* maintain accessibility.

Brand rules apply across the entire presentation.

Do not introduce unrelated visual styles.

---

## 12. Accessibility

Verify:

* readable typography;
* sufficient contrast;
* meaningful labels;
* non-color indicators;
* logical reading order;
* accessible charts;
* understandable diagrams.

Do not rely exclusively on:

* color;
* position;
* shape;

to communicate critical meaning.

---

## 13. Speaker vs Slide Content

The slide should not attempt to contain everything the presenter might say.

Distinguish between:

### Slide content

Information necessary for understanding the message.

### Speaker content

Additional explanation that supports delivery.

### Appendix

Detailed evidence or technical information that is useful but not required for the main narrative.

---

## 14. Appendix

Use an appendix for:

* detailed methodology;
* raw findings;
* technical evidence;
* definitions;
* supplementary data;
* detailed calculations;
* supporting references.

The appendix must remain consistent with the main narrative.

---

## 15. Presentation QA

Before delivery, verify:

### Objective

Is the presentation achieving its intended purpose?

### Audience

Is the level of detail appropriate?

### Narrative

Does each section logically lead to the next?

### Slides

Does each slide communicate one primary idea?

### Hierarchy

Is the main message immediately visible?

### Data

Are visualizations accurate and appropriate?

### Brand

Does the presentation comply with the available brand system?

### Accessibility

Can important information be understood without relying solely on color?

### Consistency

Are repeated patterns consistent?

### Density

Can the audience understand the slide without excessive cognitive effort?

### Evidence

Are important claims supported?

### Decision

If a decision is required, is the required action explicit?

---

## 16. Presentation Principle

**A presentation is not a collection of slides.**

It is a structured communication system designed to move an audience from understanding to an intended outcome.
