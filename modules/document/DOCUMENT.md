# Design Intelligence Pack — Document Module

## Purpose

Define how the Design Intelligence Master is applied to professional documents.

This module applies to:

- briefs;
- proposals;
- specifications;
- policies;
- procedures;
- guides;
- memos;
- internal documentation;
- knowledge documents.

---

## 1. Document Objective

Define the primary purpose:

- inform;
- instruct;
- document;
- explain;
- specify;
- persuade;
- standardize;
- enable action.

---

## 2. Audience

Identify:

- primary reader;
- secondary reader;
- expertise level;
- expected context of use.

---

## 3. Document Structure

Establish a logical hierarchy before writing.

Typical structure:

1. Title
2. Purpose
3. Context
4. Main content
5. Recommendations or actions
6. References
7. Appendix

Adapt the structure to the objective.

---

## 4. Information Hierarchy

Use:

- title;
- headings;
- subheadings;
- paragraphs;
- lists;
- tables;
- callouts;

to make information easy to scan.

Avoid excessive nesting.

---

## 5. Content

Content should be:

- clear;
- relevant;
- structured;
- accurate;
- actionable where appropriate.

Avoid unnecessary repetition.

---

## 6. Language

Adapt language to the audience.

Prefer:

- precise terminology;
- concise sentences;
- explicit statements;
- consistent terminology.

Avoid unnecessary jargon.

---

## 7. Visual Structure

Use visual hierarchy to support comprehension.

Consider:

- typography;
- spacing;
- alignment;
- tables;
- diagrams;
- callouts;
- page structure.

Visual elements must support content.

---

## 8. Tables

Use tables when structured comparison or reference is required.

Keep tables:

- readable;
- appropriately sized;
- consistently formatted;
- logically ordered.

Avoid using tables as a substitute for clear prose.

---

## 9. References

Important claims should be traceable where appropriate.

Distinguish between:

- source;
- evidence;
- interpretation;
- recommendation.

---

## 10. Accessibility

Verify:

- heading hierarchy;
- readable typography;
- sufficient contrast;
- logical reading order;
- accessible tables;
- meaningful links;
- non-color indicators.

---

## 11. Content Resilience

Test the document conceptually against:

- longer sections;
- additional headings;
- longer tables;
- missing information;
- translated content;
- revised requirements.

The structure should remain usable as content evolves.

---

## 12. Document QA

Verify:

### Purpose

Is the objective clear?

### Structure

Can readers find information quickly?

### Content

Is the information relevant and understandable?

### Consistency

Are terminology and formatting consistent?

### Evidence

Are important claims supported?

### Accessibility

Can the document be navigated and understood effectively?

### Action

If action is required, is it explicit?

---

## Document Principle

**A professional document reduces the effort required to find, understand and use information.**
